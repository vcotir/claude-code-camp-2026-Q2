# Technical Journaling

## Purpose
Technical journaling helps you document what you learned throughout the week—not just what you built, but the decisions, challenges, and conclusions from your exploration.

# Suggested Journal Structure
## Technical Goal
Briefly describe what you're trying to accomplish during the week.

## Technical Uncertainty
List any questions or unknowns before you begin experimenting.

## Technical Hypotheses
Write your educated guesses about what you expect to happen before testing.

## Technical Observations
Record important findings during your implementation.

Include:
- What worked
- What didn't work
- Unexpected behavior
- Helpful screenshots or references

## Technical Conclusions
Summarize what you learned after completing your experiments.

Include any remaining questions that you'll investigate later.

## Tips
* Keep notes concise.
* Record your assumptions before testing.
* Base your conclusions on evidence from your experiments.
* Link to screenshots, code, or documentation when helpful.

# Key Takeaway
A good technical journal captures your thought process, not just the final result. It should make it easy to understand what you explored, what you discovered, and why you made certain technical decisions.
